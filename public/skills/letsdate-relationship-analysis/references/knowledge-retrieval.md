# Retrieved Relationship Cases

Use retrieved cases only after the evidence ledger is built. They help generate alternative explanations and communication options; they do not describe the person in the current conversation.

## Hard boundary

- Never cite a retrieved case in `evidence`.
- Never say that a case similarity proves interest, personality, attachment, deception or intent.
- Never copy a case's advice when the current transcript contradicts it.
- Treat differences in culture, age, neurodivergence, workload and communication style as plausible alternatives.
- Prefer current, repeated behavior over a superficially similar case.

## How to use a match

1. Compare the case's interaction pattern with the verified fact ledger.
2. If they align, use the case to broaden the list of possible interpretations.
3. If they do not align, say that the current material does not support the pattern.
4. Turn the case takeaway into a low-pressure, observable next step.
5. Keep `knowledge` in the final report as retrieval metadata supplied by the app; do not invent extra cases.

## Short or ambiguous material

A retrieved case can justify one safe suggestion even when current evidence is thin, but it cannot justify a stable personality or attachment claim.

Always include:

- one thing that is directly observable;
- one plausible alternative;
- one practical next step;
- one question whose answer could materially change the interpretation.

Never stop at "insufficient evidence" or "wait and see".

## Source wording

The app distinguishes:

- `letsdate-synthetic`: fully fictional CC0 test scenarios.
- `cped`: anonymized aggregate patterns derived from the Apache-2.0 CPED dataset. Raw sensitive dialogue is not exposed.

The final report must preserve the supplied source and license labels.
