# Evidence Rubric

Use this rubric when deciding whether a signal is strong enough to support a personality or relationship claim.

## Source hierarchy

1. Repeated behavior across multiple chat contexts.
2. A concrete action that changes the relationship state, such as proposing a time, declining clearly, or following through.
3. One live conversational exchange with clear reciprocity.
4. Profile self-description.
5. A single isolated message.

Profile text is useful for interests, self-image, values, and possible opening topics. It is weak evidence for stable personality because users edit their profiles strategically.

## Evidence ledger

- `quote` must be an exact continuous substring of the supplied material. Paraphrase belongs in `observation`.
- Do not attribute SELF content or third-party statements to the other person.
- A repeated quote from the same original message is still one independent source.
- Keep an alternative explanation for every inference that could also be caused by politeness, context, workload, mood, culture, or platform norms.
- Cite stable evidence IDs on the fields they support. Do not use an unrelated quote merely to satisfy a schema.

## Status

- `confirmed`: the quote and direct observation are both present in the source.
- `tentative`: the observation is present, but the personality or relationship meaning has plausible alternatives.
- `insufficient`: the field cannot be supported without inventing missing context.

For personality dimensions:

- `insufficient`: fewer than two distinct relevant lines.
- `tentative`: two or three distinct lines, or one explicit self-description that directly addresses the axis.
- `supported`: at least four relevant lines across multiple contexts, with counterexamples considered.

If a host applies a later evidence minimum, follow the stricter rule.

## Confidence

- Low: one source, short material, contradictory signals, or a claim that depends on off-platform context.
- Medium: repeated patterns in chat, at least one concrete behavior, and a plausible alternative explanation.
- Higher: multiple contexts, consistent actions over time, and explicit boundary or preference statements.

Higher confidence does not mean certainty. Avoid the word "proves".

## Required cautions

Add a caution whenever:

- The evidence is a single message.
- The interpretation could also be explained by politeness, culture, work stress, neurodivergence, or platform norms.
- The trait is especially sensitive, such as emotional stability or attachment style.
- The material has no examples of conflict, rejection, or repair.
- The user may be relying on their own anxiety to connect unrelated details.

## Scoring discipline

Do not use exact scores to imply measurement precision.

- 0-24: strong counter-evidence or clear risk.
- 25-44: weak or negative signal.
- 45-54: unclear or balanced.
- 55-69: moderate positive signal.
- 70-84: strong positive signal with some limitations.
- 85-100: exceptional consistency; use sparingly.
