# Short Replies and Attachment-Related Cues

Use this reference whenever the transcript contains short replies such as "嗯", "哦", "好", "行", emoji-only messages, one-character answers, sudden message-length changes, or uneven reply timing.

## Short replies are not evidence by themselves

A short reply may mean:

- acknowledgment or agreement;
- ending the current topic;
- being busy, tired, or low-energy;
- thinking or hesitating;
- annoyance or withdrawal;
- a habitual communication style;
- lack of interest in a specific topic;
- politeness without closeness.

Do not label a person cold, avoidant, anxious, narcissistic, or uninterested from one short reply.

## Required comparison

Compare the reply against the same conversation's baseline:

- How long are the other person's non-short messages?
- How often do they normally send short messages?
- Did reply length or reply delay change recently?
- Does the short reply happen across topics or only one topic?
- Does the person answer concrete questions afterward?
- Do they re-initiate, explain, or provide context later?
- Is there a plan confirmation or commitment after the short reply?

## Classification

- `acknowledgment`: short reply followed by effective conversation or concrete action.
- `low_energy`: context supports fatigue, work, illness, or explicit low energy.
- `topic_closed`: short reply ends the current topic without broader withdrawal.
- `possible_withdrawal`: repeated short replies across contexts, reduced follow-through, and no recovery.
- `uncertain`: insufficient baseline or follow-up information.

When timestamps are unavailable, cap confidence at low unless repeated behavior is visible.

## Follow-up outcome

Every short-reply observation should state:

- `recovered`: the other person returns with a question, plan, explanation, support, or longer message.
- `no_recovery`: at least two later opportunities exist and there is still no reciprocal content.
- `unknown`: the transcript ends or there is not enough context.

## Attachment-related interaction cues

Chat can show interaction cues, not a clinical attachment diagnosis.

Analyze separately:

- Safe interaction cues: explains, repairs, respects boundaries, follows through.
- Anxious interaction cues: repeated reassurance seeking, catastrophic interpretation, fear of abandonment.
- Avoidant interaction cues: repeated withdrawal after closeness, avoidance of conflict, lack of recovery.
- Mixed or situational cues: different patterns depending on stress, topic, or relationship stage.
- Insufficient evidence: no conflict, no boundary event, no reconnection, or too little context.

Minimum requirements for a `supported` cue:

- at least three relevant behaviors across more than one context;
- visible counter-evidence;
- at least one conflict, rejection, boundary, delay, or reconnection event;
- an explanation of why the behavior is not better explained by workload, culture, neurodivergence, safety, or ordinary communication style.

Without those inputs, output `insufficient`.

## Wording

Prefer:

- "material shows some withdrawal-like interaction cues";
- "more consistent with low energy in this context";
- "I cannot distinguish a personal style from this short sample".

Avoid:

- "they are avoidant";
- "this proves they like you";
- "this proves they are manipulating you";
- "all short replies mean coldness".

## Required context gaps

If short-reply meaning is unclear, ask for:

1. The person's usual reply length and rhythm.
2. The time gap around the short reply.
3. What happened immediately before it.
4. Whether the person re-initiated later.
5. Whether they follow through on commitments.
