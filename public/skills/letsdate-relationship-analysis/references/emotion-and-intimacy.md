# Emotion and Intimacy Signals

Use this reference for every dating conversation, especially short transcripts.

## Two layers

Analyze two different layers:

1. Current emotion: good, joy, sadness, anger, fear, disgust, surprise, valence and intensity.
2. Relational intent cues: closeness, flirtation, reassurance seeking, withdrawal, control, repair, boundary negotiation and future commitment.

Do not turn one emotional expression into a stable personality or attachment diagnosis.

## Short conversations

If material is short, still provide:

- one safe, low-pressure suggestion;
- one observation that is actually visible;
- one alternative explanation;
- at least one question that would materially improve the assessment.

Never return only "insufficient evidence" or "wait and see".

## Intimacy cues

Treat these as interaction signals, not proof:

- Closeness: "miss you", "want to see you", checking whether the person got home safely, concern, care.
- Flirtation: romantic teasing, date framing, physical closeness, explicit attraction.
- Reassurance seeking: repeated checking, fear of being ignored, asking whether the other person still likes them.
- Withdrawal: short replies plus low energy, leaving the conversation, or delayed reconnection.
- Control: checking phones, location demands, reporting requirements, isolating the person, forced exclusivity.
- Repair: apology, explanation, future adjustment, reconnection.
- Boundary: asking for space, time, clarity, or slower pacing.
- Future: explicit long-term commitment or relationship definition.

Control cues override romantic interpretations. Never frame surveillance, jealousy or isolation as proof of love.

## Short read

Provide:

- a headline;
- a concrete observation;
- one actionable suggestion;
- confidence;
- missing information.

Example:

> This very short exchange contains a possible closeness signal, but it is not enough to infer attachment style. A safe next step is to respond warmly and ask one concrete, low-pressure question. More useful information would be the person's usual reply rhythm and what happened before this exchange.

## Lexicon source

The app's local emotion layer can use the MIT-licensed cnsenti DUTIR seven-emotion lexicon and HowNet modifier lists. The lexicon identifies word-level emotion cues only. Semantic meaning must still consider negation, context, sender and relationship stage.
